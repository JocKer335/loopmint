A low monthly equivalent is useful only when you also know the amount due today, the plan length, the app terms and the renewal method.

What makes up the total cost?

The monthly figure on an offer can make a plan look cheaper than the amount you’ll actually pay today. Write down the full upfront price, when access starts and when it ends. Then check whether you need a separate player app, whether activation is included and whether extra simultaneous screens cost more. A player licence and a viewing plan are two different things.

To compare plan lengths, divide the upfront amount by the number of months. That gives you a monthly equivalent, not a monthly bill. The LoopMint pricing page shows the current prices and equivalents; check it before paying because offers can change.

Your viewing plan still ends on its stated expiry date.

Compare like with like

When you compare providers, put the same details side by side: total due today, plan length, included app activation, renewal, support and refund terms. A longer plan can work out cheaper per month, but it also means paying for more time upfront. LoopMint’s one-month introductory price is for new clients; the current plan page shows the later monthly price.

Planning to watch on two screens at once? Ask whether the plan allows it. Installing the app on both devices doesn’t automatically mean the viewing plan covers simultaneous streams.

Questions to send before paying

Before you pay, get clear answers to these questions:

  • What is the full amount due today, and when does the viewing plan end?
  • Does my device need a separate player activation? What term comes with this plan?
  • How many screens can watch at the same time?
  • Do I need to request renewal, or does it happen automatically?
  • Which viewing options and support apply where I live?
