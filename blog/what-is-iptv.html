What is IPTV and how does it work?

IPTV means Internet Protocol television: television delivered through an internet connection instead of a traditional broadcast or cable feed. The label alone does not tell you who offers the content or what is included.

The four parts of an IPTV setup

IPTV can sound more complicated than it is. A typical setup has four parts:

  1. Your connection: home broadband or mobile data carries the stream.
  2. Your device: a TV, streaming stick, phone, tablet or computer plays it.
  3. A player app: this is where you sign in, browse categories and control playback.
  4. A viewing service: the provider supplies access under its own plan and terms.

Sometimes the app and service come together. Sometimes you get viewing details for a separate player. Before paying, ask which one you are being offered and what the price covers.

Live TV and on-demand viewing

Live TV works much like a regular channel: you tune in while a programme is being shown. On-demand lets you choose a film or episode from what is listed. Extras such as a programme guide, catch-up, subtitles or recording depend on the particular service, app and device.

If you have a show or match in mind, search for that title during your trial. A big catalogue number cannot tell you whether the one thing you want is available where you live, and listings can change.

What does a subscription pay for?

Before choosing a plan, make sure you know how long viewing access lasts, how many screens can watch at once, how renewals work and who to contact if something goes wrong. The player app may have its own activation period. That is separate from the viewing plan: lifetime activation for an app does not turn a two-year viewing plan into a lifetime plan.

Our plan comparison shows the activation included with each LoopMint plan. If the difference between an app and a viewing service still feels unclear, see our plain-language guide.

How to try it safely

Look for a provider that answers the basics clearly: where it serves customers, which devices it supports, what the plan costs and how renewal works. Install the recommended player from its official store, keep your login details private, then test on the screen and internet connection you will actually use. A promotional video cannot tell you how it will work in your home.
