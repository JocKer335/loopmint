How we review live TV services

Before we call a service good or bad, we need something more useful than its sales page. This is what a LoopMint review must show.

A reader should be able to tell how we reached a conclusion. Which device did we use? Where were we watching? Did we pay for the plan? And what happened when something went wrong?

We haven’t published a tested rating of a named provider. The buying and setup guides on this site are advice, and our Sling comparison refers to its official information. Neither is a substitute for a playback test. Here’s the standard we’ll use when we do publish one.

1. Record what we actually tested

A review should name the provider and website, the test date, country, device model, player app and connection. It should say whether the account was a free trial or a paid plan. Those details matter: a result on one television during one evening doesn’t establish how the service behaves everywhere.

We’ll keep three things separate: what we saw, what the provider said and what we couldn’t check. If we only read the website, we’ll say so. Screenshots can help show a menu or an error, but private account details must be hidden.

2. Check country and content claims

We’ll describe the offer we were given, including whether it covers live TV, on-demand viewing, a player licence or a combination. A player and a viewing plan can be separate purchases.

We’ll ask about the country used for the test and the programmes being advertised. A broadcaster’s logo doesn’t establish a distribution agreement. If a provider makes a licensing claim, a review must distinguish independently checked evidence from the provider’s statement.

3. Test the customer experience

We’ll start where a customer starts: requesting access, receiving the setup details and getting the app running. Then we’ll use the remote, browse the menus and try picture, sound, programme information and subtitles where offered.

An interruption needs context. We’ll record when it happened, what we were playing and which checks we tried. We won’t turn one smooth session into a promise of flawless service, or one error into a claim that everyone will have the same problem.

4. Read pricing and support terms

The amount due at checkout matters more than a large discount badge. We’ll check the plan length, simultaneous-screen rules, renewal, trial and refund terms, plus whether the player needs its own activation.

For support, we’ll send a specific setup question and record the reply and timing. A fast answer that doesn’t solve the problem isn’t especially useful; the review should explain what help was actually given.

5. Publish limits and corrections

A recommendation should explain who the service might suit, what went well and what remains uncertain. We won’t invent measurements, customer quotes or tests to fill gaps. If new evidence changes the conclusion, we’ll correct the article and date the meaningful revision.

Who publishes the guides, and how do you report a correction?

AI tools have helped draft and edit these guides. LoopMint’s plan information comes from the business, and statements about other services link to official sources. A generated draft is not evidence that a product was tested. The publisher must check service claims against its current offer before publication.

Found an outdated price, unclear instruction or wrong source? Send Sales & Setup the page link and the detail that needs attention. Leave out passwords and private access links. We keep the original publication date and show an update date when the substance changes.
